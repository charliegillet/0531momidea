# Small Business Website Outreach — Playbook

A complete plan for cold-calling small businesses, offering to build them a website,
and getting paid **only after** the site is built. This doc covers: who to target,
why, how to find them, exactly what to say on the phone, how to price it, how to
build fast, and how to avoid getting burned (since you're delivering before payment).

---

## 1. The Core Model (and its one big risk)

**Your pitch:** "I build you a simple, professional website. You only pay me after
it's live and you're happy with it. Low, flat price."

**Why it works:** Removes all risk for *them*. They say yes easily because there's
no upfront cost. Tons of small businesses still have no website, a broken one, or
just a Facebook page.

**The risk for YOU:** You do the work, then they ghost you or refuse to pay. This is
the #1 thing to defend against. Mitigations (pick a few):

- **Take a tiny deposit** ($25–$50) "to cover the domain + hosting setup." Filters
  out time-wasters and creates commitment. You can still frame the bulk as pay-on-delivery.
- **Build on YOUR hosting/subdomain first.** Show them the finished site at a temporary
  URL (e.g. `theirname.yourportfolio.com`). They pay, *then* you point it to their real
  domain and hand over access. You hold the keys until paid.
- **Simple written agreement** (even a text/email they reply "yes" to): scope, price,
  what's included, payment due on delivery. A paper trail matters.
- **Don't transfer domain ownership or admin logins until paid in full.**
- **Get the payment method ready before you start** — "What's the best way for you to
  pay once it's live, Venmo, card, check?" Smokes out non-serious leads.

## 3. How to Find Leads (and pre-qualify them)

The fastest filter: **Google Maps**. Search "plumber near [town]", look for listings
with **no website link** or one that looks broken/old. Those are your hottest leads —
they already see the need.

- **Google Maps / Google Business Profiles** — "[trade] in [town]". Note ones missing
  a website button. Phone numbers are right there.
- **Yelp, Facebook Pages, Nextdoor** — businesses with only social, no site.
- **Yellow Pages / local directories** — older businesses, often offline.
- **Chamber of Commerce member lists** — local, credible, contactable.
- **Drive around / walk your own neighborhood** — note the shops; mention you're local.
- **Instagram** — service businesses with a bio but "link in bio" going nowhere.

**Build a simple lead sheet** (spreadsheet) with columns:
`Business | Type | Phone | Has site? (no/bad/ok) | Owner name | Called? | Result | Follow-up date`

Prioritize **"no site"** and **"bad/old site"** rows. Aim for a list of 30–50 before
you start dialing so you get into rhythm.

## 4. The Phone Call — Scripts

### Mindset
- You're offering a **risk-free favor**, not begging. Confident, friendly, fast.
- Goal of call #1 is small: get a **yes to let you build a quick draft**, or book a
  5-minute follow-up. Don't oversell.
- Best times to call: mid-morning (10–11:30am) or mid-afternoon (2–4pm). Avoid lunch
  rush for restaurants, Mondays, and right before close.

### Opening (15-second version)
> "Hi, is this [business name]? Hey, my name's [Charlie] — I'm local and I build
> websites for small businesses around here. I noticed you guys don't have a website
> (or your current one looks pretty dated). I'd love to build you a clean, simple one —
> and here's the part people like: **you don't pay anything until it's done and you're
> happy with it.** Do you have 30 seconds?"

### If they're interested
> "Awesome. It'd cover the basics customers look for — your services, hours, location,
> photos, and a click-to-call button so people can reach you right from their phone.
> I'll put together a draft, send you a link to look at, and if you like it we go live.
> The cost is just [$X] flat, and only after it's up. What's the best email or number
> to send you the preview?"

### If they hesitate ("we're too busy / we have Facebook")
> "Totally get it — that's exactly why I do all the work. A website actually feeds your
> Facebook and shows up on Google when people search [their trade] in [town], which
> Facebook alone doesn't do well. There's zero cost to see a draft — if you don't love
> it, you owe me nothing. Worst case you get a free mockup. Fair?"

### Pinning down payment (do this BEFORE building)
> "Sounds good. Just so we're on the same page: it's [$X] once it's live and you've
> approved it. I'll text you a quick summary so we both have it in writing. And what's
> the easiest way for you to pay then — card, Venmo, or check?"

### Voicemail (keep it short)
> "Hi, this is [Charlie], I build websites for local businesses. I'd love to make you
> one — and you don't pay until it's done and you like it. Give me a call back at [#].
> Thanks!"

### Objection cheat-sheet
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We can't afford it." | "That's the best part — you don't pay until it's live, and it's a flat [$X], no monthly fee from me." |
| "We already have a website." | "Nice — can I take a peek? A lot of older sites don't work on phones, and 60%+ of your customers are on mobile. Happy to show you a faster version free." |
| "I need to think about it." | "Of course. How about I just build a free draft so you have something real to look at? No commitment." |
| "Who are you / is this a scam?" | "Fair question — I'm local, here's my number and a couple sites I've made: [links]. You pay nothing until it's done." |
| "We use a guy / nephew does it." | "Love that. If you ever want a second option with no risk, I'll build a free preview — keep whoever you like." |
| "Send me info." | "Will do — what's the best email? I'll also include a sample so you can see the quality." |

## 5. Pricing Strategy

You're trading low price for **volume + portfolio + reviews** early on.

- **Starter (1 page):** $100–$200. Hours, services, contact, map, click-to-call.
- **Standard (3–5 pages):** $250–$500. Home, About, Services, Gallery, Contact.
- **Add-ons (recurring = your real money):**
  - Hosting + domain management: **$10–$25/month** or $120–$250/yr.
  - Updates/edits: $25–$50 per change, or a small monthly "care plan."
  - Booking form, online menu, photo gallery refresh, SEO tune-up.

**Key insight:** The build is the hook; **recurring hosting/maintenance is the income.**
Even at $15/mo × 30 clients = $450/mo passive. Frame hosting as "I keep it online, secure,
and updated so you never have to think about it."

**Early-game move:** Do your first 3–5 cheap or free in exchange for (a) a testimonial,
(b) a Google review, and (c) permission to use it in your portfolio. That portfolio
closes every future call.

## 6. How to Build Fast (so this is actually worth your time)

Don't hand-code from scratch for $150 sites. Use builders/templates:

- **Carrd** — dirt cheap, perfect for 1-page sites (~$19/yr). Fast.
- **Webflow / Framer** — beautiful, more control, reusable templates.
- **WordPress + a theme (Astra/Kadence) + Elementor** — flexible, client can edit later,
  you can host many sites.
- **Wix / Squarespace** — easiest, but client billing is per-site; less margin for you.
- **GitHub Pages / a static template + Netlify** — free hosting, great for simple sites
  if you're comfortable with code (you've got a repo already — could template your builds here).

**Speed tactics:**
- Build **one master template** per industry (restaurant, trades, salon). Swap logo,
  colors, copy, photos. A site becomes a 1–2 hour job, not a week.
- Pull their existing **logo, photos, hours, reviews** from their Facebook/Google listing.
- Use their Google reviews as testimonials on the site.
- Always include: **click-to-call button, mobile-responsive, Google Map embed, clear
  hours, and a contact form.** That's 90% of what local customers want.
- Connect/claim their **Google Business Profile** — huge value-add you can mention.

## 7. Suggested Workflow (call → cash)

1. **Build lead list** (30–50, prioritize "no website").
2. **Call.** Get verbal yes + confirm price + confirm payment method. Send a 2-line
   confirmation text/email (paper trail).
3. **Gather assets** (logo, photos, hours, services) — or just pull from their listings.
4. **Build draft on your subdomain/staging** (1–2 hrs with a template).
5. **Send preview link.** Collect feedback, do one round of edits.
6. **Get approval → collect payment.**
7. **Go live:** point their domain, hand over what's agreed (NOT before paid).
8. **Upsell** hosting/maintenance. Ask for a **Google review + testimonial**.
9. **Ask for referrals:** "Know any other local owners who could use one?"

## 8. Quick-Start Checklist (do these first)

- [ ] Pick **2 industries** to focus on first (e.g., trades + restaurants).
- [ ] Build a **lead spreadsheet** of 30 businesses with no/bad sites.
- [ ] Set up **1 portfolio site** for yourself + 2 sample/demo sites to show.
- [ ] Choose your **build tool** + make one industry template.
- [ ] Lock your **prices** and your **deposit/payment-protection** approach.
- [ ] Write your **confirmation text template** (scope + price + pay-on-delivery).
- [ ] Make **20 calls.** Track results. Refine the script. Repeat.

---

## 9. Legal / Trust Quick Notes

- Don't promise SEO rankings or specific results you can't guarantee.
- Use **their own** photos/logo or properly licensed/free stock (Unsplash, Pexels).
- For care homes, funeral homes, medical, and churches: be sensitive, professional,
  and aware they may have approval committees (slower, but higher budget).
- Keep a simple record of agreement for every job. A reply-to-confirm text is enough
  to start.
